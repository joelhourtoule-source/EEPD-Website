IEC manufacturing standards and French installation rules

Specify equipment to its IEC manufacturing standard, then install it to French regulations: two frameworks to reconcile from the technical specification onwards.

Two complementary frameworks

Electrical equipment is designed, manufactured and tested to a product standard, usually issued by the IEC, adopted as a European standard (EN) by CENELEC and as a French standard (NF EN) by AFNOR. Its installation in France falls under another set of rules: the French Labour Code, the approved installation standards (NF C 15-100 series, NF C 13-100, NF C 13-200) and, depending on the site, specific texts on grid connection, lightning or nuclear safety. Equipment that complies with its IEC standard does not make the installation compliant: the technical specification has to bridge the two.

An example: the ITER Electrical Design Handbook

Joël Hourtoule is the author of the “Codes & Standards” part of the ITER Electrical Design Handbook (report ITR-20-005, 2020), published in open access by the ITER Organization. The document shows how to combine IEC standards and French regulations on a nuclear facility located in France:

  • IEC standards adopted wherever possible for equipment design, manufacturing and testing
  • French standards and European directives for installation and operation rules, safety and fire protection
  • An explicit order of priority in case of conflict: safety report requirements, then French safety, fire, installation and building rules, then European directives, then project documents, and finally IEC standards
  • Tables by voltage level (400 kV, 22 and 6.6 kV, 400 V) giving, for each item of equipment, the manufacturing standard, the installation rule, nuclear safety requirements (RCC-E) and tests

Frequently asked questions

Can IEC-compliant equipment be installed in France?

Yes, most IEC manufacturing standards are adopted as European EN standards and then as French NF EN standards. The installation, however, must follow French rules: Labour Code, NF C 15-100, NF C 13-100 or NF C 13-200 as applicable.

Which edition of NF C 15-100 applies?

The new NF C 15-100 series was published in August 2024. It is mandatory when the project reference date is after 31 August 2025; before that, the 2002 edition may still be used.

What is the difference between NF C 13-100 and NF C 13-200?

NF C 13-100 covers customer substations supplied by the public MV network up to 33 kV. NF C 13-200 covers the other high-voltage installations of power generation, industrial, commercial and agricultural sites.
