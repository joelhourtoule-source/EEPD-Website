Operation and maintenance: RAMI and FMECA analyses

Operation and maintenance strategy for an electrical network, based on reliability analyses (RAMI) and failure mode analyses (FMECA).

Why this study

An electrical network is judged on its availability. It depends on the design, but also on operation: periodic tests, preventive maintenance, availability of parts. RAMI and FMECA analyses focus the effort on the equipment whose failure costs the most.

From analysis to maintenance strategy

What we deliver

  • RAMI analysis (reliability, availability, maintainability, safety) of the electrical architecture
  • FMECA of the equipment: failure modes, effects, criticality
  • Maintenance and periodic test plan based on criticality
  • Spare parts and obsolescence strategy (see also our case study)
  • Organisation of the operation and maintenance team, follow-up in the maintenance tool
  • Feedback and update of the analyses

Criticality sets the maintenance policy

Input data required

  • Single-line diagrams and equipment list
  • Manufacturer data: lifetimes, maintenance frequencies
  • History of failures and interventions, if available
  • Availability targets
  • Access and outage constraints

Deliverables

  • RAMI analysis of the architecture
  • FMECA tables
  • Maintenance and periodic test plan
  • List of recommended spare parts
  • Operation and maintenance requirements for tender documents

Standards and references

The methods rely on dependability standards (IEC 60300 series, IEC 60812 for FMECA) and on the requirements of your installation.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between RAMI and FMECA?

RAMI analysis assesses the reliability and availability of the architecture as a whole. FMECA examines the failure modes of each piece of equipment and their effects, in order to rank equipment by criticality.

Should they be done before commissioning?

Ideally yes: they guide the design, the spare parts list and the operation requirements in tender documents.

Do you work on installations in service?

Yes: audit of the existing maintenance and improvement plan.
