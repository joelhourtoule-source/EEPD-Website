Emergency power supplies: diesels, UPS and DC systems

Design and operation of emergency power supplies: diesels, UPS and battery-backed DC distribution, with separate trains.

Why this study

When the normal supply is lost, important functions must stay powered. The design of the emergency sources, their separation into independent trains and their periodic tests determine real availability, much more than installed power.

Two separate trains

What we deliver

  • Identification of the loads to back up and their requirement level
  • Architecture of the emergency sources: diesels, UPS, battery-backed DC distribution
  • Separation and segregation of trains to avoid common-cause failures
  • Sizing of the sources and batteries
  • Specification of diesels: nuclear-qualified or industrial depending on the function
  • Periodic tests and maintenance strategy

Standards and references

Requirements come from RCC‑E for nuclear facilities and from the IEC 60364 series for safety services; generating sets fall under the ISO 8528 series and UPS under the IEC 62040 series.

Frequently asked questions

Why separate the trains?

To prevent a single failure, such as a fire or a control fault, from depriving all important functions of power.

What is the difference between nuclear-qualified and industrial diesels?

A nuclear-qualified diesel meets stricter qualification requirements and serves safety functions; an industrial diesel backs up functions that do not require this qualification.

What is the role of DC systems?

Batteries supply the DC distribution: control, protection, indications. They provide continuity when the normal supply is lost.
