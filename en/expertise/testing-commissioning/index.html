Factory acceptance tests and commissioning

Preparation, follow-up and validation of factory acceptance tests, site tests and commissioning of HV, MV and LV installations.

Why this study

Testing is the last moment when a design or manufacturing defect can be corrected at low cost. A test plan prepared at specification stage, with clear acceptance criteria, avoids disputes and commissioning delays.

From FAT to operation

What we deliver

  • Definition of the test plan and acceptance criteria in the specification
  • Follow-up and witnessing of equipment factory acceptance tests (FAT)
  • Preparation of site tests (SAT): equipment, functions, system
  • Tests of protection relays, interlocks and transfer to backup source
  • Follow-up of commissioning and handling of punch-list items
  • Compilation of the acceptance file

Site tests

Input data required

  • Technical specifications and manufacturing standards
  • Test plan proposed by the manufacturer
  • Single-line and control diagrams
  • Installation and outage schedule
  • Site safety requirements

Deliverables

  • Test plan and acceptance criteria
  • Factory test follow-up reports
  • Site test procedures
  • Punch list and follow-up of closure
  • Acceptance file

Standards and references

Factory tests follow product standards (for example IEC 61439, IEC 62271, IEC 60076). Verification of LV installations follows the IEC 60364 series, part 6. Safety of operations follows NF C18‑510.

Frequently asked questions

Why test at the factory?

Tests detect manufacturing and design defects before transport and installation, when correction is cheapest.

What is checked on site?

The equipment after transport, the functions (interlocks, protection, controls), then the behaviour of the system, for example transfer to the backup source.

Is personal safety taken into account?

Yes: tests follow electrical safety rules, including NF C18‑510, with authorised personnel.
