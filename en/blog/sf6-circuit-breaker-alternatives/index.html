SF6 circuit breakers: what alternatives in high voltage?

European SF6 regulation: how to plan HV extensions and study the alternatives.

By Joël Hourtoule · 31 October 2026

SF6 is an insulating gas widely used in high-voltage switchgear, but it is also a very powerful greenhouse gas. European regulation on fluorinated gases is progressively restricting the installation of new equipment, with deadlines that depend on the voltage level (check the text in force for your case; Regulation (EU) 2024/573 provides a phased SF6 phase-out between 2026 and 2032 depending on voltage level).

The steps of a strategy

  • List the existing equipment and the planned extensions
  • Study the market for SF6-free alternatives
  • Discuss requirements and possible derogations with grid operators, including RTE
  • Compare prices and take operating constraints into account
  • Define a strategy for the next extensions

Why plan ahead

Delivery and qualification lead times for alternative equipment, discussions with the grid operator and investment budgets stretch over several months, even several years. Deciding early avoids urgently installing equipment that will no longer be permitted, or locking an extension into a technology at the end of its life.

Technical points to watch

An SF6-free solution must remain compatible with the existing installation: footprint, interfaces with equipment already in place, breaking performance, maintenance requirements and training of operating teams. The comparison should not be limited to the purchase price: it includes operation and service life.
