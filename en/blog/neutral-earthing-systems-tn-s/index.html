Neutral earthing systems: TN-S and its alternatives

TN-S, TT, IT and HV neutral grounding: how earthing systems work, their advantages and how to choose for an industrial installation.

By Joël Hourtoule · 4 November 2026

TN-S, TT, IT: what the letters mean, how to choose, and why HV neutral grounding is decided together with the earthing topology.

The earthing system describes how the installation is connected to earth. It determines what happens in an insulation fault: fault current, touch voltage, whether the supply is disconnected or not. It is chosen at the start of the project, because it influences the protection plan, electromagnetic compatibility (EMC) and operation.

How to read the letters

The IEC 60364 standard designates low-voltage earthing systems with two or three letters. The first describes the source: T if the neutral point is directly connected to earth, I if it is isolated or connected to earth through an impedance. The second describes the exposed conductive parts of the installation: T if they are connected to a local earth electrode, N if they are connected to the neutral of the source. The following letters indicate whether the neutral and protective conductor are combined (C) or separate (S).

  • TN-S: neutral (N) and protective conductor (PE) separate throughout the installation
  • TN-C: neutral and protective conductor combined (PEN)
  • TT: exposed parts connected to an earth electrode separate from that of the source
  • IT: source isolated from earth or connected through an impedance, exposed parts earthed

The TN-S system

In a TN-S system, the neutral is earthed at the source, then the neutral (N) and the protective conductor (PE) run separately to the loads. The PE connects the metal exposed parts of the equipment to the source earth. An insulation fault becomes a short-circuit between a phase and the PE: it is cleared by overcurrent protection, or by residual current devices.

This separation has a practical benefit: the PE does not carry neutral current in normal operation. Exposed parts are therefore not raised to a potential by this current, which helps EMC in installations rich in electronics, instrumentation and converters. This is one of the reasons the system is chosen in nuclear and research facilities.

The other systems, briefly

  • TT: each installation has its own earth electrode. A fault does not always give enough current to trip an overcurrent device: protection is provided by residual current devices.
  • TN-C: neutral and PE are combined. Cheaper in cabling, but the PEN conductor carries load current, and it calls for EMC and disconnection precautions.
  • IT: at the first fault, the current stays low and the installation can keep running under the watch of an insulation monitor; the second fault requires disconnection. It is chosen when continuity of service comes first.

And in high voltage?

In HV and MV, we speak instead of grounding the transformer or network neutral. Three approaches dominate: isolated neutral, impedance grounding (resistor or reactor), solid grounding. The choice sets the earth-fault current, and therefore the protection settings, and it influences overvoltages on the healthy phases.

How to choose

  • Continuity of service requirement: an installation that must not stop at the first fault points towards IT
  • Presence of sensitive electronics or converters: TN-S makes EMC easier
  • Size and topology of the installation: a meshed bonding network completes the neutral system
  • Coordination with the protection plan and short-circuit studies: the earthing system sets the fault currents to clear
  • Applicable safety requirements and regulations, for example RCC‑E for nuclear facilities

Frequently asked questions

Is TN-S mandatory in a nuclear facility?

Not as a principle: the choice depends on the facility’s requirements and the applicable reference. TN-S is often chosen for its EMC advantages, as in the facilities we contributed to.

Can the earthing system of an existing installation be changed?

It is possible but heavy: protection, earthing and EMC must be reviewed. A prior study is essential.

What is the link between HV neutral and the LV system?

They are chosen together: the HV neutral sets the fault current on the source side, the LV system sets the behaviour on the load side, and both condition the protection plan.
