Electrical distribution to RCC‑E

Design and operation of electrical networks for nuclear and research facilities, in line with nuclear design rules.

The challenge

In a nuclear or research facility, the electrical supply of important functions must remain available in all circumstances. Design rules govern the separation of divisions, backup sources and operation.

What we do

  • Separation and segregation rules
  • Backup power supplies: diesels, UPS, continuous battery-backed distribution
  • Operation and maintenance based on RAMI and FMEA analyses
  • Feedback from ITER and Tore Supra

Our approach

  1. Identification of the functions and loads to be supplied from backup
  2. Definition of the architecture: separation and segregation of divisions
  3. Sizing of backup sources: diesels, UPS, continuous battery-backed distribution
  4. Reliability and maintainability analyses (RAMI, FMEA)
  5. Definition of operation and maintenance

Deliverables

  • Safety distribution architecture
  • Justification and specification files
  • RAMI and FMEA analyses
  • Operation and maintenance requirements
  • Feedback for your projects

Standards and references

Nuclear design rules, including RCC‑E, complement French electrical regulations and IEC standards. Our experience comes from ITER and Tore Supra.

Frequently asked questions

Is RCC‑E reserved for nuclear power plants?

RCC‑E is a design code for the electrical systems of nuclear facilities; it can serve as a reference for other research or safety facilities, depending on the operator’s requirements.

What backup equipment do you study?

Diesels, UPS and continuous battery-backed distribution.

What do RAMI and FMEA mean?

RAMI: reliability, availability, maintainability and inspectability. FMEA (AMDEC in French): failure modes, effects and criticality analysis.
